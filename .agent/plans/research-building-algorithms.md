# Building-Generation Algorithms — Research Reference

Deep-research notes on procedural building-modeling algorithms, anchored on the
straight skeleton (which powers this addon's hip/gable roofs via
`btools/utils/util_skeleton.py`, adapted from polyskel / Felkel & Obdržálek
1998). Each family below includes mechanism, building applications, key papers,
Python/Blender availability, and the open issues it unlocks here.

Written 2026-09, post mathutils skeleton refactor (commit `bf2ee8e`).

---

## 0. Where the straight skeleton sits

The straight skeleton is one member of a *wavefront-propagation* family.
Relatives generalize it along three axes:

- **weights** — per-edge propagation speed (per-wall roof pitch)
- **distance metric** — L-infinity/mitered (skeleton) vs Euclidean (medial axis)
- **input generality** — holes, planar straight-line graphs, curved boundaries

Orthogonally, buildings are generated by **grammars** (rules), by
**partitioning/optimization** (layouts), and by **data-driven reconstruction**
(GIS/ML). Sections 1–4 cover each.

---

## 1. Skeleton family (closest relatives)

### 1.1 Weighted straight skeletons

Each polygon edge propagates at its own speed; vertices travel along *weighted
bisectors*. Multiplicative weights scale speed (CGAL: offset line of
`ax+by+c=0` at time t is `w(ax+by+c)-t=0`); additive weights add constant
lag/lead; negative weights move edges outward. With strictly positive weights
the skeleton behaves combinatorially like the unweighted one.

**Building applications**

- per-wall roof pitch: sheds, half-hips, mansard breaks, clerestory shapes
- CGAL `extrude_skeleton(pwh, mesh, angles=..., maximum_height=...)` —
  angle-per-tent input and flat-top truncation = exactly a mansard control
- asymmetric setbacks (one plot edge recedes faster than another)

**Papers**

- Aichholzer, Aurenhammer, Alberts, Gaertner 1995, "A novel type of skeleton
  for polygons", J. UCS — origin, roof interpretation
- Eppstein & Erickson 1999, "Raising roofs, crashing cycles, and playing pool",
  DCG 22(4)
- Biedl, Held, Huber, Kaaser, Palfrader 2015, "Weighted Straight Skeletons in
  the Plane", Comput. Geom. Theory Appl. 48(2) — the rigorous definition
  (arbitrary weights need planar-matching event handling)
- Eder & Held 2018, IPL 132 — positively weighted simple polygons
- Held & Palfrader 2017, "Straight Skeletons with Additive and Multiplicative
  Weights and their Application to the Algorithmic Generation of Roofs and
  Terrains", CAD — the roof-generation paper

**Availability**: CGAL 5.6+ `Straight_skeleton_2` (C++/GPL, not
Blender-callable); Surfer2 (github.com/cgalab/surfer2, research C++);
polyskel has no weights but multiplicative positive weights are a formula
swap in our Felkel simulator (bisector construction + event distance).

**Complexity**: positive multiplicative weights in-tree — moderate (~1–2
weeks incl. edge cases). Additive/negative — hard (leave to CGAL).

**Unlocks**: #208 (hip roof by angle/percentage) — CGAL's
`extrude_skeleton(angles=...)` is the feature checklist to emulate.

### 1.2 Medial axis transform (MAT)

Locus of centers of maximal inscribed disks (Blum 1967); equals the interior
Voronoi diagram of the boundary. Edges are line segments AND parabolic arcs
(vs the skeleton's all-lines). Coincides with the straight skeleton for convex
polygons; diverges only at reflex vertices.

**Building applications**

- true distance-based insets: `buffer(-t)` is the MAT level set — wall
  thickness, hollowing, rooms that vanish exactly when corridor width < 2t
- centerline extraction: roads, rooms (Haunert & Sester 2008, GeoInformatica)
- corridor/circulation generation: Explicit Corridor Map (van Toll et al.,
  SoCG 2016 demo); peripheral-MAT circulation networks (SAGE 2024)
- "distance roofs" with curved valleys (lift MAT by radius)

**Papers**: Blum 1967; Lee 1982 (IEEE TPAMI, O(n log n) simple polygons);
Sherbrooke et al. 1996; Tagliasacchi et al. 2016, "3D Skeletons: A
State-of-the-Art Report", CGF 35(2).

**Availability**: scipy.spatial.Voronoi on densely sampled contours
(~100–200 LOC + radius/angle pruning); skimage.morphology.medial_axis (raster,
Lee–Kashyap–Chu thinning); Shapely negative buffer for distance insets.

**Complexity**: sampling+Voronoi — low. Exact segment-Voronoi — hard.

### 1.3 Skeletons with holes / multi-contour / non-simple inputs

Wavefront spawns from outer contour and each (oppositely-oriented) hole; hole
fronts expand while the outer shrinks; skeleton gains one cycle per hole.
CGAL additionally inserts *artificial bisectors* post hoc to re-cut ring faces
so offset extraction stays valid. Exterior skeletons use the frame trick
(polygon as hole of a huge rectangle). Non-simple/PSLG inputs are
Surfer2/CGAL territory.

**Unlocks**: #103 (floorplan holes) — polyskel already accepts clockwise hole
contours; the roof side needs finishing + hardening (split events between
contours are the pain point).

### 1.4 Polygon offsetting (mitered vs Minkowski)

- **Mitered offsets come free from a skeleton** (Palfrader & Held 2015, CAD&A
  12(4)) — one skeleton yields offsets at every distance with topology changes
  handled by construction. Setbacks, eaves/overhangs, wall-thickness insets,
  **mansard multi-level offsets** (stacked insets, each extruded).
- **Rounded/beveled joins**: Minkowski sum with a disk — Shapely `buffer()`
  (fast, vector-clean, resolution-bound) or pyclipper (integer-exact
  Vatti clipping, robust for repeated boolean chains; raise MiterLimit for
  skeleton-like corners).

**Unlocks**: PR #230 / #40 mansard tiers; setback options; wall thickness.

### 1.5 Roof-model bag of tricks

- **Laycock & Day 2003** ("Automatically generating roof models from building
  footprints", WSCG): derive hip / gable / half-hip / mansard from ONE
  skeleton by post-processing which faces reach the ridge. Pure
  post-processing on what we already compute — cheapest #40 win.
  https://www.sthu.org/misc/SKRW14/papers/LaycockDay_2003_AutomaticallyGeneratingRoofModelsBuildingFootprints.pdf
- **Kada cell decomposition** (PhoWo 2007/2009 — the 3D Berlin engine, 474k
  buildings): cluster near-parallel footprint edges into averaged cut lines,
  partition into mostly-quad cells, instance named roof primitives
  (flat/shed/gable/hip/pyramid) selected by LiDAR regression-plane normals,
  with dedicated junction primitives. The industrial LoD2 alternative to
  whole-footprint skeletons; no skeleton dependency.
  https://phowo.ifp.uni-stuttgart.de/publications/phowo09/350Kada.pdf
- **Robustness**: the Felkel–Obdržálek algorithm is *proven incomplete*
  (multi-edge/vertex events). **bpypolyskel** (github.com/prochitecture/
  bpypolyskel) is a Blender-native bmesh/mathutils port hardened for ~320k
  OSM hipped roofs (99.99% success) with portable fixes: node merging,
  ghost-edge filtering, apse multi-edge events. Relevant to #149 (hip roof
  failure for complex polygons).
- Jing et al. 2021, "Intuitive and efficient roof modeling for reconstruction
  and synthesis", SGP/Computer & Graphics — modern interactive roof work.

### 1.6 Exotica (offline/CGAL territory)

- **Alpha wrapping** (CGAL Alpha_wrap_3): guaranteed watertight shrink-wrap
  hulls of arbitrary/defective input — LOD generation, messy-mesh cleanup.
- **Wavefront quad-meshing** (QuADMESH): boundary-aligned re-quadtilateralation.
- **Kinetic triangulations** (Palfrader, Held, Huber, ESA 2012): the robust
  modern skeleton engine (Surfer2) — handles all event degeneracies.

## 2. Grammar family

| Grammar | Mechanism | Best for | Min-version effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-systems (Parish & Mueller 2001) | parallel rewriting + global goals/local constraints | streets, lots; weak for buildings | skip for buildings |
| Shape grammars (Stiny 1972) | geometric subshape rewriting | style-true plans | research-only; skip |
| Split grammars (Wonka 2003) | box → boxes; repeat/float splits + control grammar + attribute matching | facades | 1–2 weeks |
| CGA shape (Mueller 2006 / CityEngine) | scopes + comp splits + float/repeat splits + skeleton roofs + setbacks + seedian | everything building-side | 2–4 weeks (80/20 subset) |
| Predicate grammars (Smelik 2014 taxonomy) | rules guarded by semantic queries | control layer | days — we already have one |
| Inverse procedural (Talton 2011; Nishida 2018) | fit grammars to images/clouds | facades from photos | park it |

Notes:

- **CGA core ops** mirror things this addon has or wants: `comp(f){front:
  FrontFacade | side: SideFacade | top: Roof}` = our world-space selectors,
  industrialized; `split(y){4: Groundfloor | {~3.5: Floor}*}` = storey/bay
  layout; `roofGable/roofHip` = straight skeleton + inset (CGAL machinery
  under the hood — Esri is a CGAL customer); `setback`/`shapeL/O/U` = massing;
  `seedian` + `rand()`/`p()` = seeded deterministic randomness.
- **Eger & Mote 2022** (FDG, "Instant Architecture in Minecraft using
  Box-Split Grammars") implement split-grammar rules as plain Python
  functions over labeled boxes — the cheapest implementation pattern for us.
  https://slothlab.info/assets/pdf/eger2022fdg.pdf
- **Schwarz & Mueller 2015**, "Practical grammar-based procedural modeling of
  architecture" (SIGGRAPH Asia course) — the best single practical reference
  for reimplementing this family.
  https://michael-schwarz.com/research/publ/files/procmodcourse-siga15.pdf
- Our selector + facade-rule + matgroup architecture is effectively a
  predicate grammar over a semantically annotated model (Smelik et al. 2014,
  CGF 33(6)) — cite when documenting the design.
- **Seeded hierarchical RNG ("seedian" pattern)**: one root seed per building,
  `rng = random.Random(f"{seed}:{decision_path}")` per decision node →
  reproducible hashes, per-branch re-roll, stable reference diffs. Unlocks
  #163. Effort 2–4 days.

## 3. Layout & partitioning family (interiors, facades)

1. **BSP / squarified treemaps / slicing floorplans** — recursive splits under
   an area program; zone-then-room double application is the published recipe
   (Marson & Musse 2010; "Procedural Generation of Traversable Buildings"
   2015). ~200 LOC, no deps. Best fit for rooms + interior walls.
2. **Skeleton + convex decomposition** — skeleton arcs as corridor spines
   (Honeybee's "straight skeleton → core/perimeter sub-faces" component is the
   published pattern); Hertel–Mehlhorn convex decomposition (≤4x optimal,
   ~150 LOC) pre-splits L/T footprints before BSP refinement.
3. **Adjacency graphs + MST doors** — networkx/Prim over the room tree for
   minimal door placement preserving reachability (+ preset-specified
   adjacencies). Days.
4. **MIP / CP-SAT constraints** (OR-Tools; GRIDS CHI 2020) — hard guarantees:
   equal bays, aligned lintels, clearances, egress. Optional "strict mode",
   fail-soft to greedy.
5. **WFC / model synthesis** — hierarchical WFC masked to the footprint +
   path constraint (HWFC floor-plan repo is the best open reference);
   grid-quantized so needs vector snap-to-wall. Differentiated "organic
   interiors" mode, later. BorisTheBrave's WFC tips are the practitioner bible.
6. **Learned floor plans** — RPLAN dataset (80k+ plans), House-GAN/House-GAN++
   (Nauata 2020/21), Graph2Plan 2020, HouseDiffuser CVPR 2023. Torch-in-Blender
   is a non-starter; use OFFLINE to mine room-area/adjacency priors and bake
   them into presets as tables.

Facade-side: strip/shelf packing (NFDH/FFDH) over storey bands + alignment
snap; symmetry-by-construction (grids) rather than detection; Poisson-disk
constrained scattering for irregular ornament.

## 4. Urban & data-driven family (GIS/batch)

- **Tensor-field streets** (Chen et al. 2008): grid/radial tensor basis,
  Laplacian diffusion of user strokes, streets as eigenvector
  hyper-streamlines. For fictional context only; otherwise consume real
  graphs via OSMnx.
- **Parcel subdivision** (Vanegas et al. 2012, EG): skeleton-offset perimeter
  strips (every lot fronts a street) + recursive OBB interior splitting —
  both reduce to straight-skeleton computation.
- **Setbacks = polygon offsetting** (see 1.4); **solar envelopes** =
  inclined-plane half-space intersections from minimum sun angles
  (Knowles; Alkadri et al. 2020 taxonomy) — cheap first pass as a ridge
  height clamp `h_max = d * tan(alpha_min)`.
- **OSM Simple 3D Buildings** is a ready-made attribute schema:
  `building:part` polygons, `height` (ground-to-rooftop, excl. antennas),
  `min_height`, `building:levels` (3 m/level fallback), and a 30-value
  `roof:shape` vocabulary (flat, gabled, hipped, half-hipped, side_hipped,
  mansard, gambrel, skillion, saltbox, sawtooth, butterfly, pyramidal, cone,
  dome, onion, round, ...) plus `roof:height` / `roof:angle` /
  `roof:orientation=along|across` / `roof:direction` (ridge defaults parallel
  to longest side). Essentially a free requirements doc for the roof engine.
- **LoD ladder** (CityGML; 3D BAG): LoD1 prism → LoD1.3 per-part heights →
  LoD2.2 semantic roof/wall/ground surfaces. Our matgroup tagging is already
  a semantic-surface layer — a CityJSON exporter would be nearly free.
  Reference pipeline: Peters et al. 2021, "Automated 3D reconstruction of
  LoD2 and LoD1 models for all 10 million buildings of the Netherlands"
  (arXiv:2201.01191).
- **Benchmark**: blosm (Blender OSM importer) does hipped roofs on quads
  only — real skeleton hips on arbitrary N-gons is our differentiator.

Practical batch pipeline (for #173/#214): ingest (osmnx/geopandas) → local
ENU meters (pyproj) → clean footprints (shapely make_valid/simplify/snap) →
OBB orientation snap → height cascade (height tag → levels x 3 m → class
prior → block median → nDSM sample) → decompose outline vs building:part →
roof:shape → skeleton/profile roof families → btools api build → matgroup
validate → optional terrain (DTM sampling, stepped foundations).

## 6. Ranked adopt-first list

1. **Seeded hierarchical RNG (seedian)** — <1 week; unlocks #163 +
   deterministic reference hashes.
2. **Laycock–Day roof-style post-processing** — one skeleton → half-hip/
   mansard/gable variants; pure post-proc for #40/PR230.
3. **Multiplicative weighted skeleton** — per-wall pitch for #208; constrained
   formula swap.
4. **Skeleton-derived mitered offsets** — mansard tiers, setbacks, wall
   insets; reuses the existing skeleton.
5. **bpypolyskel robustness patches** — #149-class failures on complex
   footprints.
6. **Split-grammar facade DSL (CGA subset)** — the facade multiplier; biggest
   long-term item.
7. **Treemap/BSP + skeleton corridors + MST doors** — the #202/#217 stack.
8. **OSM/LoD ingestion pipeline** — #173/#214; section 4 is the spec.
